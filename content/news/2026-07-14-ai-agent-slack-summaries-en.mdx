An AI agent for Slack summaries does one of two jobs. Slack's own AI *catches you up* — it recaps a channel, thread, or your unread messages so you don't scroll. A separate agent *acts on it* — routing what matters, drafting a reply, or filing action items into a tracker. Catching up isn't acting.

## Two jobs hiding in "summarize my Slack"

The word "summarize" makes it sound like one feature. But the moment you ask *what happens after the summary*, it splits. One job ends when you've read the recap — you're caught up, and that's the whole point. The other job starts there: someone, or something, has to decide the summary means *reply to the client, flag the outage, log the decision.* Slack's built-in AI is very good at the first job. The second is where an agent earns its name. It's the same split we hit with [email management](/en/news/2026-07-07-ai-agent-email-management/) — a feature *shows* you the pile; an agent *does something* about it.

## Job 1 — Catch up (Slack's built-in AI)

This is now baked into Slack. Per Slack's [guide to AI features](https://slack.com/help/articles/25076892548883-Guide-to-AI-features-in-Slack), you can summarize a channel, a thread, or a direct message — choosing just your unread messages, the last seven days, or a custom range. **Recaps** go a step further: an automated morning summary of the unread channels you keep an eye on but don't post in, so you skim one digest instead of opening ten channels.

The pricing story changed in 2025. Slack [retired the standalone Slack AI add-on and folded AI into its paid plans](https://slack.com/blog/news/june-2025-pricing-and-packaging-announcement): conversation and thread summaries come with any paid plan, while the daily Recaps tier is [reserved for Business+ and Enterprise+](https://slack.com/help/articles/39264531104275-Updates-to-feature-availability-and-pricing-for-Slack-plans). If your workspace is already on a paid plan, you may have summaries switched on and not know it.

The honest limit: this is a read tool. It tells you what happened; it doesn't touch anything. That's the same boundary we hit with Claude's Google Workspace connectors in [Issue #001](/en/posts/2026-05-13-gmail-ai-agent/) — great at reading, deliberately hands-off on acting.

## Job 2 — Act on the summary (an agent)

When you want the summary to *lead somewhere*, you've left native-Slack territory. Two honest routes:

- **No-code automation.** Tools like [Zapier's Slack-summary workflows](https://zapier.com/blog/summarize-slack-threads/) trigger on a new message or a schedule, run an AI step to summarize, and post the result somewhere — a DM, another channel, a doc. The summary becomes an *event* that fires an action, which native Slack summaries never do.
- **A dedicated Slack agent.** Third-party agents such as [Read AI](https://www.read.ai/slack) sit inside Slack and push summaries and action items across channels and meetings. These go past recap into "here's what you now owe people."

None of these replace the built-in recap — they consume it. A common shape: let Slack AI catch you up, then let an agent turn the two lines that actually matter into a task, a draft, or a ping to the right person. Treat it like any real [AI agent](/en/agent-101/): read what it decided before you trust the send, because a confident summarizer can still misread who owns the action.

## Which do you actually need?

If your pain is *"I spent an hour scrolling #general to see what I missed,"* that's Job 1 — turn on Slack's summaries and Recaps; you may already pay for them. If your pain is *"things get decided in Slack and then dropped,"* that's Job 2 — you need an agent that moves the outcome into wherever work actually gets tracked. Most teams need Job 1 first, and only reach for Job 2 once the firehose is understood but still unactioned. ## FAQ

**Can AI summarize my Slack channels?**
Yes — it's built into Slack's paid plans. Slack's [AI features](https://slack.com/help/articles/25076892548883-Guide-to-AI-features-in-Slack) can summarize a channel, thread, or DM (unread, last seven days, or a custom range), and **Recaps** deliver an automated daily digest of channels you follow. Slack [folded these into paid plans in 2025](https://slack.com/blog/news/june-2025-pricing-and-packaging-announcement) instead of selling a separate add-on.

**Is Slack AI free?**
No, but it's no longer a separate purchase. After Slack's [2025 pricing change](https://slack.com/help/articles/39264531104275-Updates-to-feature-availability-and-pricing-for-Slack-plans), summaries come with paid plans (Pro and up), and daily Recaps are included on Business+ and Enterprise+. There is no Slack AI on the free plan.

**What's the difference between a Slack summary and a Slack agent?**
A summary *reads* — it tells you what a channel or thread said. An agent *acts* — it routes the summary, drafts a reply, or files an action item into a tracker. Native Slack AI does the first; a [no-code automation](https://zapier.com/blog/summarize-slack-threads/) or a dedicated agent does the second.

**Can I make Slack summaries trigger an action automatically?**
Not with native Slack AI — it's you-initiated and read-only. For automatic behavior you route Slack messages through an automation like [Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/summarize-slack-threads/) or a [no-code agent platform](/en/news/2026-07-08-no-code-ai-agent-tools/) that can summarize and then post, file, or notify on a trigger.

**Do I need code to build one?**
No. Slack's summaries are a toggle; the "act on it" layer can be built with no-code tools, the same way as the [Gmail triage agent](/en/posts/2026-05-13-gmail-ai-agent/) in Issue #001. Code only enters when you need triggered behavior no platform exposes.
