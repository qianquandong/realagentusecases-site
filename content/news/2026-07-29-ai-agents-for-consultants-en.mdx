AI agents help consultants across separable jobs: gathering and synthesizing research, and drafting the deck, model, or proposal. The big firms built their own — McKinsey's Lilli, BCG's Deckster. What none of them should own is the recommendation itself: the integrative judgment a client pays for stays human.

## The consulting workflow, and which parts an agent can take

Consulting is a pipeline: research the problem, synthesize it into a story, and advise the client on what to do. An AI agent is genuinely useful for the front of that pipeline — pulling sources, summarizing prior work, drafting the slides and the model — because a consultant reviews everything before it reaches a client. The part to keep human is the *advice*: the read of a specific situation that carries the engagement's value and its risk. So the honest way to test any "AI agent for consulting" pitch is to ask, at each stage, *does the agent do the work, or make the call?* It's the same automate-the-work-not-the-decision line we drew for [recruiters](/en/news/2026-07-23-ai-agents-for-recruiters/), [marketers](/en/news/2026-07-27-ai-agents-for-marketers/), and [accountants](/en/news/2026-07-28-ai-agents-for-accountants/) — the professional-services pattern holds here too.

## Research — synthesize the inputs, don't skip the reading

The front of an engagement is reading: prior decks, market data, expert calls, the client's own documents. This is where the big firms pointed their first agents. [McKinsey rolled out Lilli](https://www.ciodive.com/news/McKinsey-generative-AI-Lilli-platform-internal-employees/691231/), an internal assistant that pulls from the firm's own body of past work and proprietary research so a consultant can synthesize findings without re-reading every source by hand. This is the safest stage to automate because the output is checkable — every claim traces back to a document you can open. The [Harvard–BCG field experiment](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/) found that on tasks *inside* AI's capability frontier, 758 BCG consultants using GPT-4 completed 12.2% more tasks, finished 25.1% faster, and produced results rated over 40% higher quality than a control group. Research synthesis is squarely inside that frontier — as long as a person still verifies the sources the agent leaned on.

## Draft — build the deck and the model, keep your name on the logic

Consultants live in slides, and drafting them is the second job agents took over inside the big firms. [BCG built Deckster](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bernardmarr/2024/07/10/how-bcg-is-revolutionizing-consulting-with-ai-a-case-study/), a slide assistant trained on the firm's templates that drafts and grades presentations, alongside GENE, a GPT-4o-based partner for brainstorming and content. At the enterprise end, [Deloitte launched Zora AI](https://www.cio.com/article/3848252/deloitte-unveils-agentic-ai-platform.html), an agentic platform of ready-to-deploy agents for finance, HR, and other back-office functions — closer to a digital workforce than a drafting aid. The individual consultant's version is smaller but the discipline is identical: let the agent turn your research into a first-draft deck or model, then own the logic and the numbers before anyone sees them. It's the same draft-vs-publish split we mapped for [content repurposing](/en/news/2026-07-24-ai-agent-for-content-repurposing/) — automate the draft, keep a hand on what ships.

## Advise — the recommendation stays human

Here the same [Harvard–BCG study](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf) draws the line for you. The researchers deliberately included a task engineered to sit *outside* AI's frontier — one that required weighing conflicting evidence and reaching a nuanced business judgment. On that task, consultants using GPT-4 were *more* likely to reach the wrong conclusion than consultants working without it: confident, well-formatted, and incorrect. That's the recommendation stage. An agent that synthesizes cleanly can still lead you to a plausible answer that's wrong for the client in front of you, and it has no stake in being right. The deliverable a client actually buys — "here is what you should do, and why, given your specific situation" — is the judgment that sits outside the frontier and stays with the consultant.

## FAQ

**What AI agents can help consultants?**
Match the agent to the stage. For research, a synthesis agent — the desk-level cousin of [McKinsey's Lilli](https://www.ciodive.com/news/McKinsey-generative-AI-Lilli-platform-internal-employees/691231/) — summarizes sources into a checkable brief. For drafting, an agent turns that research into a first-draft deck or model, the way [BCG's Deckster](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bernardmarr/2024/07/10/how-bcg-is-revolutionizing-consulting-with-ai-a-case-study/) drafts slides. The recommendation itself stays with you. Start with whichever stage costs you the most time.

**Can an AI agent write my consulting recommendation?**
It can draft the deck around it, but the recommendation should stay human. The [Harvard–BCG field experiment](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier/) found that on a task requiring integrative business judgment — the shape of a real recommendation — consultants using GPT-4 were more likely to reach the *wrong* conclusion than those without it. AI is strongest on the research and drafting inside its frontier, and weakest on exactly the call a client pays for.

**Do McKinsey and BCG really use AI agents internally?**
Yes. [McKinsey rolled out Lilli](https://www.ciodive.com/news/McKinsey-generative-AI-Lilli-platform-internal-employees/691231/) as an internal assistant grounded in its own research, and [BCG built Deckster and GENE](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bernardmarr/2024/07/10/how-bcg-is-revolutionizing-consulting-with-ai-a-case-study/) for slides and brainstorming; [Deloitte launched Zora AI](https://www.cio.com/article/3848252/deloitte-unveils-agentic-ai-platform.html), a platform of deployable agents. These are internal, enterprise tools — study the pattern, but reproduce it at desk scale.

**Do I need to be a big firm to use consulting AI agents?**
No — and that's the point. Lilli and Deckster run on infrastructure an independent consultant doesn't have, but the *pattern* (research synthesis, deck drafting, human judgment) is reproducible with tools you can run yourself. **Where should a solo or boutique consultant begin?**
With the stage that eats the most hours — usually the reading. Start with a research-synthesis agent that turns sources into a brief you verify, then add deck drafting.
