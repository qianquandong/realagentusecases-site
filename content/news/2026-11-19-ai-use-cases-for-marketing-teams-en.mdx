AI use cases for a marketing team are the everyday tasks a person can hand to Copilot, ChatGPT, or Claude today: draft a blog post, email, or ad; repurpose one asset into channel versions; summarize market and competitor research; and pull a campaign report. AI produces the draft; a person owns the publish button.

Most "AI for marketing" advice is either a tool roundup or a list of what's theoretically possible. What a team actually needs is smaller: the two or three everyday tasks to start with this week, framed so the work stays reviewable. This is the marketing-team entry under the site's broader [department-by-department map](/en/news/2026-11-17-department-ai-use-cases/) — the concrete tasks, not a product shortlist.

## The everyday tasks to start with

Every vendor that documents this lands on the same short list of marketing jobs, and they're all some version of *draft it, find it, summarize it, report it*. Here's the starting set, each tied to where it's documented.

- **Draft the first version.** Blog posts, emails, ad copy, landing-page sections, product descriptions. OpenAI's own [marketing use-case guide in OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/public/resources/use-cases-marketing) covers brand narratives, creative concepts, and campaign copy, and notes a reasoning model suits strategic brainstorming. A first draft is the single highest-volume thing a marketer can delegate — and the one with the clearest review step.
- **Repurpose one asset into channel versions.** Turn a single blog post, webinar, or report into a LinkedIn post, a newsletter blurb, and a short-video script. This is its own documented pattern on the site — [draft vs. publish for content repurposing](/en/news/2026-07-24-ai-agent-for-content-repurposing/) and [the distribution step](/en/news/2026-07-25-ai-agent-for-social-media-content/) — where the tool adapts the format and a person approves what ships.
- **Summarize research.** Pull recent market trends, competitor activity, and customer sentiment into a brief. Microsoft's [Copilot Scenario Library for marketing](https://adoption.microsoft.com/copilot-scenario-library/marketing/) documents exactly this — a marketer asking Copilot to gather trends and competitor activity, then draft from the material — plus campaign brainstorming in Teams and social scheduling in Planner.
- **Report on a campaign.** Ask a plain-English question of the numbers — top-performing channels, click-through, conversions by region — in a spreadsheet instead of building the pivot by hand. Microsoft's scenario library includes a post-launch marketing performance report; the general version is [getting an answer out of a spreadsheet](/en/news/2026-08-20-ai-agent-for-data-analysis/).

Notice what's shared: the task differs, the shape doesn't. AI does the legwork — the draft, the first pass, the summary — and a marketer keeps the judgment.

## What stays a human's job

The line that keeps a marketing team out of trouble is the same one this site draws everywhere: AI drafts, a person publishes. The reason is specific to marketing. A draft goes out under your brand, makes claims a customer will act on, and can't be unsent once it's live. So the review step isn't optional polish — it's where a person checks the voice, verifies every factual claim and number against a real source, and catches the confident-but-wrong line before it ships.

That's why the useful framing isn't "automate marketing," it's *draft vs. publish*. Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude are good first-draft engines; they are not publishing engines. The deeper tool-by-tool view of where agents fit the marketing workflow lives in the [AI agents for marketers](/en/news/2026-07-27-ai-agents-for-marketers/) hub — this page is just the shortlist of what to start with. Keep the publish button with a person, and the rough early outputs that come with any new tool get caught instead of mailed.

## Turn it into one measurable start

A list of tasks is not a plan. Pick **one** — the highest-volume, most repetitive job on the list, usually first-draft copy or repurposing — and run it like a small [AI pilot](/en/news/2026-11-05-how-to-run-an-ai-pilot-at-work/): set a baseline, give it a few weeks, and decide whether it earned a place in the workflow. Then [measure whether it's actually used](/en/news/2026-10-22-how-to-measure-ai-adoption-at-work/), not whether the license was bought.

Starting with one task is also how adoption sticks: a team that wins on first drafts comes back for repurposing on its own. ## FAQ

**What are good AI use cases for a marketing team?**
The everyday ones: drafting first versions of blog posts, emails, ad copy, and landing-page sections; repurposing one asset (a blog post, a webinar) into channel-specific versions; summarizing market, competitor, and customer research into a brief; and reporting on a campaign by asking plain-English questions of the numbers. OpenAI's marketing use-case guide and Microsoft's Copilot Scenario Library for marketing both organize the work this way. The common thread is that AI produces the draft or the summary and a person owns the publish button.

**Should AI write our marketing content on its own?**
No — treat it as a first-draft engine, not a publishing engine. A marketing draft goes out under your brand and makes claims a customer acts on, so a person has to check the voice and verify every factual claim before it ships. The gain is speed to a draft, not hands-off publishing.

**Which marketing task should we start with?**
The highest-volume, most repetitive one — usually first-draft copy or turning one asset into channel versions. Pick a single task, run it as a short pilot with a baseline, and only add the next one once the first clearly helps. A long list of use cases at once mostly spreads attention thin.

**Is this different from buying a marketing AI agent?**
Yes. Most of these starting tasks are just a marketer using an assistant well — drafting, summarizing, reporting. A multi-step [agent](/en/agent-101/) that researches, drafts, and posts on its own is a later step, and it needs a human-approval gate once it can publish or send. Start with the assisted task; graduate to an agent only when the workflow clearly justifies it.

**Where do these use cases come from?**
Vendors' own catalogs — the [Microsoft Copilot Scenario Library](https://adoption.microsoft.com/copilot-scenario-library/marketing/), [OpenAI Academy's marketing guide](https://academy.openai.com/public/resources/use-cases-marketing), and OpenAI's [identifying-and-scaling use-case guide](https://openai.com/business/guides-and-resources/identifying-and-scaling-ai-use-cases/) — cross-checked against this site's own documented builds for content work, so each task is one marketers are actually running.
