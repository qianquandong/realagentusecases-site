AI agents help financial analysts across separable jobs: gathering research and data, and building the model or analysis. Named tools — Morgan Stanley's Assistant, AlphaSense, Microsoft Copilot Finance, Rogo — automate the legwork. What none of them should own is the call itself: the investment or business judgment a stakeholder acts on stays human.

## The financial-analyst workflow, and which parts an agent can take

Financial analysis is a pipeline: gather the inputs (filings, market data, prior models), build them into a model or an analysis, and advise — a valuation, a forecast, a buy/sell or a budget call. An AI agent is genuinely useful for the front of that pipeline — pulling filings, extracting the numbers, drafting the model — because an analyst reviews everything before anyone allocates capital against it. The part to keep human is the *call*: the read of a specific situation that money and reputation ride on. So the honest way to test any "AI agent for financial analysis" pitch is to ask, at each stage, *does the agent do the work, or make the call?* It's the same automate-the-work-not-the-decision line we drew for [accountants](/en/news/2026-07-28-ai-agents-for-accountants/), [consultants](/en/news/2026-07-29-ai-agents-for-consultants/), [lawyers](/en/news/2026-08-03-ai-agents-for-lawyers/), and — before any of them — for [inbox triage in Issue #001](/en/posts/2026-05-13-gmail-ai-agent/).

## Gather — pull the filings and data, don't skip the read

The front of the job is reading: 10-Ks, earnings-call transcripts, market data, sell-side notes. This is where the first agents landed, because the output is checkable — every figure traces back to a filing you can open. [AlphaSense's Deep Research](https://www.alpha-sense.com/blog/product/ai-uplevel-financial-research/) is purpose-built for financial workflows: given a question it runs dozens of searches, parses thousands of results, and reasons over them to produce an investment-grade briefing — work the company frames as a team of analysts operating at "superhuman speed." Its CEO Jack Kokko [told CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/10/wall-street-analyst-now-ai-ceo-on-why-stock-pickers-wont-go-extinct.html) the point plainly: "AI isn't replacing analysts, it's giving them superpowers." Inside the banks, [Morgan Stanley](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/18/morgan-stanley-chatgpt-financial-advisors.html) was the first major Wall Street firm to put a bespoke GPT-4 tool in employees' hands — the AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant gives advisors fast access to a library of roughly 100,000 research reports and documents, and its companion [Debrief](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch) summarizes client meetings and surfaces action items. Gathering is safe to automate precisely because a person can check the source behind every claim.

## Model — build the analysis, keep your name on the numbers

Analysts live in spreadsheets, and drafting the model is the second job agents took over. [Microsoft 365 Copilot Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/finance/welcome), [generally available since October 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics-365/blog/it-professional/2025/10/20/empowering-finance-with-an-ai-assistant-in-microsoft-365-copilot/), runs variance analysis on pivot tables from natural-language criteria, reshapes raw ERP exports into analysis-ready tables, and generates formulas and visualizations inside Excel — the mechanical middle of building a model, done for review. Purpose-built platforms push further: [Rogo](https://rogo.ai/) generates financial models, analyzes SEC filings, and summarizes earnings calls, and its agent Felix lets a banker delegate a task by email "the way they would to a junior analyst" and get finished work back. Rogo says more than 35,000 professionals at over 250 institutions — Lazard, Jefferies, Moelis and others — use it, and it [raised a $160M Series D in April 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/04/29/rogo-raises-160m-speed-financial-analysis-ai-agents/). The discipline is identical to the [accountants' record layer](/en/news/2026-07-28-ai-agents-for-accountants/): let the agent draft the model, then own the assumptions and the numbers before anyone acts on them. The same tools now power everyday spreadsheet work beyond finance — our [AI agent for data analysis](/en/news/2026-08-20-ai-agent-for-data-analysis/) guide takes the desk-level, any-spreadsheet version of this step.

## Advise — the call stays human

Here the line is sharpest, because a valuation or a forecast is the thing someone allocates capital against. The [CFA Institute's January 2026 analysis](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/investor/2026/01/05/ai-in-finance-changing-workflows-growing-demand-for-human-judgment/) — titled, pointedly, "Growing Demand for Human Judgment" — reads AI as augmenting rather than replacing analysts, and warns that heavy reliance on these systems may dull the cognitive capabilities that underpin robust investment judgment. It also cites the practical ceiling: advanced AI agents autonomously complete only about 30% of complex, multi-step tasks, so the machine's output still needs a person to fact-check it. That maps to exactly the desk-level, reproducible pattern behind our [headline-vs-desk-level examples](/en/news/2026-07-17-real-ai-agent-examples/): copy the workflow, not the autonomy. The deliverable that carries the analyst's name — "here is the number, and here is why, given these assumptions" — is the judgment that stays human.

## Which agent should a financial analyst start with?

Start where your work actually stalls. If you drown in filings and earnings calls before every model, begin at the gather layer — a research-synthesis agent that turns sources into a checkable brief, built the desk-level way, not the Morgan Stanley way. The reproducible pattern is the same one behind [Issue #001's Gmail triage agent](/en/posts/2026-05-13-gmail-ai-agent/): a bounded task, a checkable output, no platform team required. If model-building eats your day, [Microsoft Copilot Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/finance/welcome) sits inside the Excel you already use. New to agents entirely? Begin at [Agent 101](/en/agent-101/), or pick a builder from the [no-code AI agent tools](/en/news/2026-07-08-no-code-ai-agent-tools/) guide. Whatever you start with, hold the line: automate the gathering and the model, own the call.

## FAQ

**What AI agents can help financial analysts?**
Match the agent to the stage. For gathering, a research agent like [AlphaSense Deep Research](https://www.alpha-sense.com/blog/product/ai-uplevel-financial-research/) or an internal assistant like [Morgan Stanley's](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/18/morgan-stanley-chatgpt-financial-advisors.html) pulls and summarizes filings and reports. For modeling, [Microsoft Copilot Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/finance/welcome) runs variance analysis in Excel and [Rogo](https://rogo.ai/) drafts financial models. The investment call stays with you. Start with whichever stage costs you the most time.

**Can an AI agent build my financial model?**
It can draft one — [Rogo](https://rogo.ai/) generates financial models and [Copilot Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/finance/welcome) does variance analysis and formulas — but you own the assumptions and the numbers before anyone acts on them. Treat the model the way an accountant treats a reconciliation: a reviewable draft, not an autonomous decision, the same [record-vs-sign-off line](/en/news/2026-07-28-ai-agents-for-accountants/) we drew for accounting.

**Will AI agents replace financial analysts?**
Not on the current evidence. The [CFA Institute](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/investor/2026/01/05/ai-in-finance-changing-workflows-growing-demand-for-human-judgment/) reads AI as augmenting rather than replacing analysts, notes that advanced agents autonomously complete only about 30% of complex multi-step tasks, and argues the technology *increases* the value of human oversight. AlphaSense's CEO frames it the same way — "giving them superpowers," not replacing them. The judgment is the job that stays.

**Do I need to work at a bank to use these?**
No. Rogo and the Morgan Stanley Assistant are enterprise tools, but the *pattern* — gather, model, human judgment — is reproducible at desk scale. [Microsoft Copilot Finance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/finance/welcome) runs inside Excel, and the [no-code AI agent tools guide](/en/news/2026-07-08-no-code-ai-agent-tools/) covers builders you can wire a research or data step into yourself.

**Where should a solo analyst or a student begin?**
With the stage that eats the most hours — usually the reading. Start with a research-synthesis agent that turns filings and notes into a brief you verify, the bounded-task, checkable-output setup from [Issue #001](/en/posts/2026-05-13-gmail-ai-agent/), then add model-drafting on top as you trust it.
